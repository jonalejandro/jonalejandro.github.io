How I calculate it

I use steady aerobic laps near 145 bpm and estimate equivalent pace after accounting for route grade and climate. Grade is modeled from the route elevation profile with the Minetti energy-cost curve. Climate uses temperature, dew point, humidity, and modeled wind exposure from nearby weather observations. Qualifying laps last at least 10 minutes, have an average heart rate of 140–150 bpm, and exclude the first lap. Warmups, short recordings, threshold sessions, trail runs, indoor runs, and altitude or travel runs are excluded.

Calibration: speed is normalized to 145 bpm using the initial fitted heart-rate correction, grade is converted to energetic cost with the Minetti curve, and climate is estimated from observed temperature, dew point, humidity, and wind. Corrected speeds are averaged by qualifying-lap duration. The climate calculation is a transparent estimate, not COROS’s unpublished implementation. The trend is an unweighted linear fit of included run pace against date, scaled to 30.44 days; it is not a forecast.

Grade + climate pace estimates an equivalent effort under normalized terrain and weather conditions. Fatigue, sleep, fueling, cardiac drift, surface, and weather-station distance can still move an individual point, so the multi-week trend matters more than any single run.
